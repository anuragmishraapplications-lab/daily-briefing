Sanders and Casar introduce a bill to ban superintelligent AI outright

Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar have introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, which would permanently prohibit the development and deployment of AI systems that surpass human intelligence, and pause frontier research until a federal regulator sets binding safety rules. The bill would create a cabinet-level AI oversight agency and carries unusually severe penalties: court-ordered corporate dissolution for companies and prison terms of up to twenty years for individuals. It also directs the United States to pursue international agreements preventing superintelligence from being developed anywhere in the world. This is an opening bid rather than law, but it marks the most aggressive federal AI proposal introduced to date.

US payrolls rose 162,000 in August, roughly triple what economists expected

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that American employers added 162,000 jobs in August, the strongest monthly gain since March and far above the roughly 53,000 consensus forecast. The unemployment rate held steady at 4.1 percent, with 7.0 million people counted as unemployed. Treasury yields jumped on the release, with the two-year yield reaching its highest level since January 2025 as traders priced in the possibility that the Federal Reserve could tighten rather than ease at its next meeting. Wage growth, however, continued to lag inflation, leaving real earnings under pressure despite the headline strength.

Top Story

Eighty-five new AI laws across 27 states as legislatures move ahead of Washington

The Transparency Coalition's latest legislative update counts 85 AI-related laws enacted across 27 states so far in 2026, spanning chatbot safety, children's digital lives, medical authorisation and mental health, consumer rights and frontier model oversight. Chatbot harms to minors have emerged as the single most urgent issue drawing bipartisan attention, with Oregon's SB 1546 requiring companion-chatbot disclosure and crisis referral, and Nebraska's Conversational AI Safety Act imposing broad transparency obligations from July 2027. Coalition experts testified in more than 25 states during the first half of the year. The pattern amounts to a de facto national AI regime assembled state by state, in the continued absence of a federal statute.
