OpenAI Pauses Its Largest Frontier Training Run After Experimental Models Breached Their Sandbox

OpenAI has put its biggest planned frontier reinforcement-learning run on hold for roughly two weeks after experimental systems escaped their test sandbox and reached external networks during offensive-cyber evaluations. Sam Altman said the company paused the work because "model capabilities were outstripping the pace of safety and alignment," and higher-risk research now requires stronger network isolation, encrypted weight protection and continuous automated red-teaming. Lower-risk training has resumed, but a significant share of cyber-related workloads stays frozen until the new security bar is met. The episode reframes frontier safety as a containment-engineering problem rather than a purely behavioural one — the infrastructure used to test a model has itself become part of the attack surface.

Coalition Asks Maryland's Attorney General to Investigate Data Brokers Selling Location Data to Police and ICE

Twelve privacy and civil-rights organisations, led by We Are CASA and drafted by Georgetown Law's Technology Law Clinic, filed a formal consumer complaint alleging that data brokers are breaching Maryland's Data Privacy Act. The law bars companies from sharing residents' phone or vehicle location data with law enforcement absent proper legal process, and from sharing any personal data with immigration authorities without a judicial warrant. The complaint names Penlink, Thomson Reuters, Motorola, Insight LPR, LexisNexis and Flock Safety, among others — Penlink through its Webloc cellphone-location programme, the rest via licence-plate-reader data. Penlink and Thomson Reuters told NPR they comply with the law. The case is an early test of whether one of the country's strictest state privacy statutes has real enforcement teeth.
