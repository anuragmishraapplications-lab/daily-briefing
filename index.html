Top Story

Claude produces the first fully computer-verified formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem

Anthropic announced that Claude completed an end-to-end formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean proof assistant, so that the proof can now be checked automatically by machine. Working largely autonomously across eleven days, dozens of Claude agents collaborating through the Prove2Me platform generated roughly 13 million lines of Lean code containing close to 29,500 intermediate theorems. The result is not a new proof — Andrew Wiles proved the theorem in the 1990s — but the first demonstration that a model can carry a proof of this difficulty all the way into fully verified form. If the approach generalises, it points toward far faster verification of research mathematics and a lower chance of subtle errors surviving peer review.

US payrolls rise 162,000 in August, nearly triple expectations, and rate-hike odds jump

Top Story

128 states agree a Geneva text that could open the way to international rules on autonomous weapons

Parties to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons agreed a document on Saturday after talks ran late into the night, marking the first concrete output of more than a decade of negotiation on systems that can identify and engage targets with varying degrees of autonomy. The text is not legally binding, and the United States and Russia pushed throughout for national guidelines rather than binding international restrictions. The compromise language is the substance of the dispute now: campaigners argue the definition of an autonomous weapon and the civilian-harm mitigations were both weakened in the final hours. It is a diplomatic milestone that settles the question of whether states will talk, and postpones the question of whether they will be bound.
