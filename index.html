Top Story

China’s August imports miss forecasts as pressure to rebalance trade builds

Chinese exports rose 25 percent year on year in August in dollar terms while imports climbed 28.2 percent, short of the 30 percent economists expected. The trade surplus widened to $119.09 billion from $112.5 billion in July, extending a run of record-scale imbalances. Economists and foreign officials are increasingly pointing to an undervalued yuan as the mechanism sustaining the export boom, and calling on Beijing to let the currency strengthen. Domestic demand remains the soft spot: China is still leaning on exporters to carry growth, which is precisely what makes the surplus politically combustible abroad.

Top Story

EU AI Act enters its first wave of compliance inspections

The transition period for general high-risk systems closed on 2 August 2026, and through September the European AI Office in Brussels begins scheduled compliance inspections alongside 24 national market surveillance authorities. France’s CNIL, Germany’s BfDI and Spain’s AESIA are directing their first information requests at three sectors: automated CV screening in human resources, algorithmic credit assessment in retail banking, and AI triage tools in private healthcare clinics. This is the point at which the Act stops being a drafting exercise and becomes an enforcement regime, and the first requests will set the template for how aggressively the rest of the bloc reads the high-risk category.
