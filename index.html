OpenAI says an internal model produced a proof for the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize problem

OpenAI announced that a system materially more capable than its just-released GPT-6 Astra generated an analytical proof that a three-dimensional fluid governed by the Navier–Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time — a question open for roughly ninety years. The company said around 10,000 coordinating agents ran for 88 hours to reach the result. The claim is not yet independently verified, and NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster, who was pursuing related work with Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge, has publicly questioned whether OpenAI accelerated its effort after learning of their progress. If the proof survives peer review it would be the first Millennium Prize problem substantially resolved by machine.

Top Story

UK bill to force AI crawlers to identify themselves reaches second reading

The Automated Online Software (Access and Transparency) Bill, a Private Member's Bill sponsored by Damian Hinds MP and backed by the News Media Association, sits at second reading in the Commons in the 2026–27 session. It would create a registration regime for operators of bots, crawlers and scrapers that systematically access UK websites, requiring them to declare their identity and the purpose of their activity. The proposal responds to a roughly 300% rise in overall AI bot traffic during 2025 and an almost 8,000% increase in agentic AI traffic, which publishers and online retailers say has made content extraction effectively untraceable. If enacted it would be one of the first national frameworks to regulate automated access as a distinct governance problem rather than as a copyright dispute.
