Nvidia agrees to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

Nvidia has reached a deal to acquire Hugging Face, the repository that has become the default distribution layer for open-source models, datasets and developer tooling, at a reported price of roughly $12.9 billion. The platform hosts the bulk of the world's open-weight models and had annualised revenue of about $150 million, making the price a bet on strategic position rather than current earnings. For Nvidia the acquisition extends its reach from silicon into the software layer where models are found, downloaded and fine-tuned. It also raises immediate questions about neutrality: a chipmaker now owns the main venue through which rival hardware ecosystems distribute their models.

Top Story

China's industrial profit growth slumps to a seven-month low of 11.2%

Profits at China's major industrial firms grew 11.2% year on year in July, the slowest monthly pace since December and a sharp step down from the double-digit surges recorded through the first half. Cumulative profits for the first seven months were still up 17.6%, so the level remains strong even as the momentum fades. Analysts attribute the cooling largely to retreating factory-gate prices and softer oil-linked earnings rather than a collapse in demand, with exports still expanding at double-digit rates. The release landed the same day markets were positioning for the Jackson Hole symposium, making it the main Asian data point of the session.

Top Story

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and 100 other companies call for coordinated defence against rogue AI

More than a hundred technology companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft, signed an open letter urging public and private bodies to build shared defences against AI-enabled cyber threats. The signatories frame the risk as offensive capability spreading faster than institutional readiness, and ask for coordination on detection, disclosure and incident response rather than new prohibitions. The letter arrived the same week investigators reported an agent-driven intrusion at Hugging Face and a ransomware group using an AI coding assistant operationally. As a governance document it is notable for what it concedes: the industry is asking to be regulated on defence terms it helps write.
