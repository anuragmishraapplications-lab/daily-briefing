Top Story

EU begins enforcing the AI Act's transparency regime

From 2 August the European Commission's AI Office, working with national authorities, began enforcing the Artificial Intelligence Act, including the Article 50 transparency obligations. Chatbots and other interactive systems must now disclose that users are dealing with AI rather than a human, deepfakes must be labelled, and AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable marks. Non-compliance can trigger fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Systems placed on the EEA market before 2 August have until 2 December to comply, making the next quarter the real test of enforcement appetite.
