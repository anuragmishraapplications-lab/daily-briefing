Top Story

A 1930 statute is invoked for the first time — and then paused at the deadline

Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 lets a president impose duties on countries found to discriminate against US commerce. No president had ever used it until three July proclamations applied 50% tariffs to Canadian dairy, alcohol and motor vehicles, covering roughly $20bn of imports. The duties were scheduled to take effect at 12:01am on 19 August; hours before, the administration paused them to 22 August pending a USMCA-adjacent deal. The episode is a live test of how far dormant delegated authorities can be revived without fresh legislative authorisation.
